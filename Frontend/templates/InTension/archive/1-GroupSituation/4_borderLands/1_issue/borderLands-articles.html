Voicesfrom theBorderland

Opening invitational notes on the Borderland

Geographically, culturally, politically, epistemologically, the borderland is where difference meets with the potential for exchange. Culturally the borderland can be reflected in language, food, music, inter-marriage, or tension and friction when relations are historico-politically inflected, and in other ways. Then there are the more complex relations which are not spatially grounded but arise from the divide between the colonised and the (contemporary or historical) coloniser. Here the borderland can be an immaterial divide of hostile entrenched positions. Equally it can be the locus of a postcolonial project of reconciliation, material exchange, and the making of new relationships out of a process of decoloniality—based on a mutual recognition of, and respect of, cosmological and epistemological difference. This enables the exploration of the underpinning values, beliefs, and practices of the respective cultures and their modes of knowledge, while recognizing the consequences of the imposition of the knowledge of the coloniser on the colonised in the past, and how it continues in the present. Notwithstanding historically created conditions of limitation, this situation is one in which exchange of knowledge can begin to take place. Realistically, such relations take time and generational change. But they need to be woven together, when there are connections already occurring.
