The illusionanddangersoftheextendedmind

Mind is a locus of contestationequal to, or greater than,the environment.

Mind is a product of the relation of the brain and the ontological designing of what is external to it. One example of this is a change in the nature of reading. Functional (how-to) reading is increasing, while to learn, cultivate the mind, and gain pleasure from reading decreases. This marks a more general move from 'deep to shallow engagement' linked to transition from a general literary cultural environment to a media consumptive one of instant entertainment — a situation being seen not just as reducing memory, but intellectually damaging the ability to think and write beyond instrumental needs. There is also an increase of dependence on the means of tertiary retention (devices of artificial memory) which effectively produces a ‘poverty of mind’ from reduced use of mental analytic and reasoning skills, while diminishing the condition of imagination (the informed facility to image from experiential encounters in the world) which prompts the imagined. It follows that artificial means that think for us (AI) reduce this developmental process.

Tech-corporate productivist instrumentalism appears blind to the ontological designing of mind of the products they produce (variously described as ‘brain rot,’ ‘digital dementia, and ’the diminishment of cognitive abilities’). What is underway is a condition of intellectual slippage in which, increasingly, AI thinks for many of us. This is, in effect, a trend that can lead to the power of mind slipping into technological control (singularity). The power of AI and its links to quantum computing can engender a feeling of ignorance, and an already recognised danger of loss of control (and the designation of ‘our’ redundancy). The speed of this technology development is alarming, and this speed is going to increase. ‘We’ may well be between a world of human and technological dominance. Notwithstanding the global inequity of the distribution of the digital technosphere, the impacts on us are going to be huge. There is growing recognition of the economic displacement of vast numbers of industrial and administrative workers (already named as ‘the useless class’ ). Some of the creators of AI have even warned of it destroying ‘us.’
